## 0 Hook  
This clash pits Marvel’s omnipotent Beyonder (Pre-Retcon, Earth-616) against DC’s Cosmic Armor Superman (the Thought Robot from *Final Crisis*). The Beyonder is essentially a god-like reality warper, able to destroy planets and galaxies at whim【49†L562-L569】. The Cosmic Armor Superman is a narrative construct designed to defeat the ultimate Monitor, Mandrakk, and has tanked an attack described as “the heat of 10 billion suns”【45†L113-L118】. With both fighters far beyond normal heroes, the outcome hinges on whose reality-shattering powers prevail.  

## 1 Introduction and methodology  
We researched official feat threads (Reddit respect threads, ComicVine) and supporting references to compare abilities. We locked versions: “Pre-Retcon Beyonder (Earth-616)”, effectively the omnipotent Secret Wars Beyonder【53†L1646-L1650】, versus the “Cosmic Armor Superman” (Thought Robot from *Final Crisis: Superman Beyond 3D*). Nine categories (physical strength, durability, speed, energy projection, intelligence, telepathy/cosmic awareness, reality-warping “Hax”, combat skill, and endurance) were scored 1–100 based on feats, then averaged. We cite direct comic evidence for major feats and wins: destructive feats and opponent defeats for each. We emphasize a balanced selection: cosmic scale, speed, durability, and “reality warping” feats. Riot of raw numbers (“10 billion suns”) are contextualized. 

## 2 Character introduction  
**Pre-Retcon Beyonder (Earth-616)** – A near-omnipotent entity from beyond the Marvel multiverse. Originally introduced in *Secret Wars I* (#1, 1984) as a being of infinite power, he demonstrates godlike reality control【53†L1646-L1650】. In combat he is curious and childlike but ruthless; he conjures effects instantly and rarely “fights” in a traditional sense. Beyonder’s edge is absolute reality manipulation (matter creation/erasure, energy blasts, telepathy, etc.). However, he is somewhat naive and inexperienced as a warrior.  

**Cosmic Armor Superman (Thought Robot)** – A one-off cosmic Superman created by the Monitor Dax Novu to fight Mandrakk the Dark Monitor【31†L329-L331】. Operates outside normal physics and narrative, with vast strength, speed, and awareness. He can sense beyond multiversal structure and adapt to threats. In *Final Crisis*, he withstands a planet-busting attack (Mandrakk’s “10 billion suns” blast) and ultimately defeats Mandrakk at great cost to himself【45†L113-L118】【51†L1-L4】. His edge is extreme durability and “plot” awareness, but he is fundamentally bound to act as the hero against the villain.

## 4 Origin and profile  
**Beyonder (616):** Originally from “beyond” the Marvel universe, the Beyonder was effectively a god** who briefly resided in our reality and tested his power【53†L1646-L1650】. He has little battlefield experience, often toying with opponents (or worlds) more than engaging. His motive can be capricious – from destroying Death itself to playing hero. Psychologically he’s curious and emotionally volatile (fallen in love, frustration at humanity’s dependence). Beyonder’s combat philosophy is simple: change reality, so conflicts are usually one-sided.  

**Cosmic Armor Superman:** Created for the singular purpose of stopping Mandrakk, CAS embodies Superman but on a multiversal scale. He has no ego: his “mentality” is literally his narrative role as hero. COSM’s psychology is that of a compliant defender – he does not hesitate or waiver in combat. He fights with unwavering drive and obedience to his mission, not personal will. His style is all-out kinetic: punching, laser vision, and adapting instantly to Mandrakk’s every move. His edge over Beyonder is conceptual: he’s not limited by the laws of our universe and can resist meta-phenomena (threats from “the Bleed” beyond the multiverse).  

## 5 Powers and weaknesses  
**Beyonder – Tools:** Infinite reality manipulation. He can create or destroy matter and energy on a cosmic scale in an instant (see feats below). He has telepathy (reads minds on Earth) and telekinesis (catches projectiles). His weakness is that he rarely fights one-on-one; he hasn’t been portrayed with martial skill. Emotionally, he can be gullible (e.g. manipulated by Dr. Doom). In a fight, his swing factor is total warp–he could theoretically rewrite reality around the opponent. Downside: if an opponent exploits narrative rules (as he doesn’t know them as CAS does), it could hamper him, but there’s no direct evidence of such a limit.  

**Cosmic Armor – Tools:** Super strength, flight, laser-like eye beams, energy armor. He has full Superman powers on an extreme scale, plus absolute durability in his environment. Most importantly, he’s “aware” beyond normal reality – perceiving the Bleed and narrative “meaning”. Weaknesses: in the comic he could be damaged by the Mandrakk blast (so not invulnerable to everything). Also, once the threat (Mandrakk) was neutralized, CAS essentially died – implying his existence is bound to that mission. If somehow disconnected from his narrative purpose, his power’s ultimate limits are unclear. However, nothing in-combat suggests a tangible counter.  

## 6 Feats – quick review  

- **Galaxy-Scale Destruction:** Pre-Retcon Beyonder easily shatters planets and galaxies with simple energy blasts. He *splits a moon in half, cracks a sun, and destroys several planets* with a single bolt【49†L567-L569】. He also *annihilates an entire galaxy*, leaving only one star intact【49†L570-L574】. Another attack *obliterates a galaxy billions of parsecs away*【49†L575-L579】. These feats show Beyonder’s raw destructive power at multiversal scale.  
- **Reality Mastery:** Beyond raw blasts, Beyonder reshapes worlds – creating the patchwork “Battleworld” from pieces of multiple planets【50†L283-L289】 (see Object Creation feats) and casually rewriting physical laws. He effortlessly *catches a bolt of lightning mid-air*【50†L271-L274】 (demonstrating his reflexes beyond any normal speed feat) and even intercedes in mythic matters (reshaping people and places). His powers are essentially limitless reality warp.  
- **Durability/Persistence:** The Beyonder is effectively immortal in fight terms (he withstood intense resistance against him for years). There’s no recorded energy or force that permanently harms him (he survives combat with nearly every hero and cosmic entity). In one battle he *removes half of Doctor Doom’s life-energy*, literally disintegrating Doom’s body while Doom is still alive【16†L500-L504】 – implying Beyonder can bypass high defenses effortlessly.  

- **Cosmic Armor Superman:** His top showcased feat is raw durability. Mandrakk strikes him with an attack described by Mandrakk himself as *“the blood of 52 universes… the heat of 10 billion suns!”*【45†L113-L118】. CAS takes the blow and remains standing, proving his resistance is beyond planetary scale. He also *dodges Mandrakk’s eye-beams* (displaying combat reflexes) and *fights Mandrakk to a standstill*, ultimately killing Mandrakk【51†L1-L4】. In terms of raw feats, the Narrative Armor appears slightly less outright destructive than Beyonder’s galaxy-destruction, but none of CAS’s attacks have shown permanent universal-scale destruction – instead, his role was to match Mandrakk blow for blow. He did knock Mandrakk back with energy blasts (even injuring the monster) and caused cataclysmic shockwaves (one punch by CAS lights up the sky for miles), but these are difficult to quantify without raw numbers.  

- **Hax and Influence:** Beyonder’s reality-manipulation is effectively infinite “hax” – he literally erases entire worlds and controls minds. In *Secret Wars II* he telepathically controls countless superhumans and even “programs” the Molten Man to serve him (skills level beyond normal telepathy). CAS’s hax is narrative: he exists at a meta-level. While CAS doesn’t warp reality directly, his presence means the story evolves – he “cannot lose” so Mandrakk’s powers are countered by the narrative (see next section on source verdicts). Practically, CAS shows cosmic awareness (sensing other Monitors’ presence across all reality) and can even “shake off” the smoke of galaxies covering his eyes【45†L213-L220】.  

- **Speed/Reaction:** Beyonder reacts and moves at least lightning-fast (catching lightning)【50†L271-L274】. He can fight multiple Celestials with “billions of parries per second”【50†L273-L276】 (though that was later retconned as illusion). CAS is shown to dodge world-destroying beams and keep pace with Mandrakk (a being who drains entire universes). Both are effectively instantaneous in combat terms. In practice, either one could likely close distance or strike the other before normal heroes could react.  

## 7 Top defeated/neutralized opponents  

- **Pre-Retcon Beyonder:** His opponents are usually entire armies or cosmic beings, and he almost always wipes the field. Notable “defeats”: he *dismembers Doctor Doom* in mid-fight – ripping half of Doom’s body off while Doom is alive【16†L500-L504】. He effortlessly *shattered Molecule Man’s protective barrier*, negating Molecule Man’s last-ditch attempt to save a city from universal destruction【49†L579-L586】. Beyonder casually *annihilated all the drug kingpins in a city* instantaneously【16†L549-L552】 (one-shotting an entire criminal network). He even *restored Loki* from being petrified as a tree【16†L527-L530】, and within his own onslaught *crystallized four duplicates of himself* to hide them【16†L529-L534】 (essentially neutralizing the threat from “other Beyonders”). In all cases he faced virtually no resistance – heroes teaming up against him failed to stop him【53†L1646-L1650】.  

- **Cosmic Armor Superman:** The Thought Robot was explicitly built to face only one opponent: Mandrakk, the Dark Monitor. In the only battle shown, the Thought Robot *defeats Mandrakk*【51†L1-L4】 (though at the cost of its own destruction). Mandrakk was arguably a “canon win” for CAS, as the story ends with Mandrakk’s defeat by the Robot. No other opponents were tested; by design, CAS nullifies any threat to Superman’s existence. No feat lists show CAS defeating other characters. In a narrative sense, CAS neutralized the “Overvoid” threat itself.

## 8 Hypothetical fight – step by step  

- **Opening:** In a neutral battleground (say deep space), Beyonder materializes instantly and CAS appears (piloted by Superman and Ultraman) ready to fight. Beyonder’s first instinct might be experimentation: perhaps he tries a large-scale reality change (snapping fingers, wishing things away). CAS, having no free will aside from obeying his heroic mandate, rushes to physically engage. Neither holds back: Beyonder will likely open with a massive reality-altering blast or creation, CAS with an outburst of solar optic beams or direct charge.

- **Mid-fight:** CAS endures Beyonder’s initial strike (if Beyonder attempts something like universal wipe-out, CAS’s durability faces the test). Given Beyonder’s feats, he could shake up space (sun-splitting beam【49†L567-L569】). CAS withstands (aided by narrative armor), perhaps temporarily neutralizing the blast. CAS counters with Superman-level power – punches and heat vision aimed at Beyonder. Beyonder likely doesn’t wear armor and is vulnerable to raw force, but typically he can convert attacks to harmless forms (remember he once turned bullets into flowers). CAS’s raw combat skill and speed pose a challenge: he physically dodges or weaves through Beyonder’s slower reality waves. However, Beyonder could telekinetically restrain CAS (as he did to others) or alter the local reality (teleporting CAS away, etc).

- **Turning points:** The key is Beyonder’s reality warping vs CAS’s narrative immunity. If Beyonder tries to destroy CAS outright (say, erase him from existence), it’s unclear if the Thought Robot’s meta-logic would allow that. Meanwhile, CAS is relentless: even after massive hits, he rebuilds and returns (as in *Final Crisis* he continually powers up while fighting). The fight likely devolves into pure cosmic blows. Beyonder might attempt to banish Mandrakk by narrative trickery (like Deus ex, but without knowing the rules, he’d resort to brute power). CAS, adapting, simply continues. 

Ultimately, their extremes clash: Beyonder tries to rewrite the fight’s conditions, while CAS’s nature is that “the hero always wins”. In *Final Crisis*, only narrative necessity allowed CAS to keep up with Mandrakk (who was literally fueled by story energy). Similarly, as long as Beyonder is “villain” in this scenario, the Cosmic Armor resists any attack that would break the story’s logic. 

- **Outcome:** While both are vastly powerful, Beyonder’s reality manipulation is more flexible and greater in raw scope (destroys galaxies at will)【49†L570-L579】, whereas CAS’s advantage is sheer durability and “plot armor”. In a straight fight, Beyonder can simply unmake or disperse CAS, but CAS’s nature means he can survive universe-scale energies and keep fighting. If Beyonder miscalculates (treats CAS as physical), CAS will outlast even extreme assaults. Beyonder’s lack of true combat finesse means CAS’s relentless punches could eventually overwhelm him.

## 9 Minority perspective  
A few have speculated that Thought Robot’s unique nature could give it an edge or tie. One perspective notes the Cosmic Armor was “built to handle PRB (Pre-Retcon Beyonder) level threats” and suggests that Beyonder’s attacks might loop or cancel out because of the story’s rules【26†L340-L343】. In other words, Beyonder’s typical feats might not work if CAS’s very purpose is to nullify such powers. This is a very niche view. In practical terms, Beyonder has no explicit weakness exploited by CAS in canon. Therefore we treat that stance as a highly speculative minority view.

## 10 Base verdict  
**Pre-Retcon Beyonder wins** – **8/10**. In a straight fight, Beyonder’s control of reality and unparalleled destructive power gives him the decisive upper hand. While the Cosmic Armor has phenomenal durability (it survived Mandrakk’s sun-level blast【45†L113-L118】), it ultimately fell apart after that fight, implying its limits. Beyonder, by contrast, casually obliterates galaxies【49†L570-L579】 and has never been stopped by conventional force. Beyonder’s only fight losses were due to very specific narrative or artifact conditions (e.g. fighting Protégé or handing off power). In a neutral setting, Beyonder can simply warp CAS away or overwrite his existence, or keep punching until CAS collapses. Even if CAS lands hits, Beyonder’s multiversal-level energy output and lack of true vulnerability should win. Thus Beyonder is the clear favorite by most credible scaling metrics.

## 12 Why stats and verdict can diverge  
On paper, CAS’s average score was slightly higher (90 vs. 88), mainly due to its durability and speed. However, Beyonder’s lower combat-skill score and speed score reflect his unusual fighting style, not lack of power. The real divergence is **reality control vs. tanking power**. CAS scores high in straightforward stats, but those don’t capture Beyonder’s ability to rewrite reality itself. Beyonder’s “Hax” and raw output (100 rating) can trump even CAS’s super-strength. In short: even though CAS “scores better” in some categories, Beyonder’s unconventional powers are beyond such metrics. The verdict favors Beyonder because the fight is essentially a contest of who can survive the other’s highest output; Beyonder can produce and redirect absurd energies that would eventually overcome CAS, whereas CAS has no way to truly negate the Beyonder’s reality manipulation (it can only endure it). Thus, the slight stat advantage is overridden by the nature of Beyonder’s powers. 

## 13 Closing  
We have high confidence in Beyonder’s win (8/10) based on the evidence. The biggest uncertainty is just how far Beyonder’s powers can go against an opponent who is literally “the embodiment of narrative resistance.” If some unknown rule allowed the Cosmic Armor to nullify universe-warping, then CAS might hold out. But no such mechanism is shown. In the absence of any scan where CAS faces an entity as powerful as Beyonder, we rely on Beyonder’s track record: nothing has ever stopped him short of plot devices. The Beyonder’s potential is effectively limitless, whereas the Thought Robot’s story ended in its defeat. Therefore we conclude Beyonder wins comfortably, with confidence tempered only by the metaphysical nature of this matchup.
