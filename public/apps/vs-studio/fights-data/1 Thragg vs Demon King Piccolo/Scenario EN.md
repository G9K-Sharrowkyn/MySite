Hey there. I'm G9K Rubikon, and I love spending whole days breaking down fights between different pop-culture characters.

Originally, the first fight was supposed to be a different one, and it had already been ready for about three weeks, but the latest episode of Invincible hit me too hard not to change the order.

So today we're taking a look at Thragg, Grand Regent of the planet Viltrum, and Demon King Piccolo.

To start, what are we going to estimate... Stats.
Battle IQ - how intelligently character A uses their abilities IN THE FIGHT ITSELF,
Durability - how much damage they can take from opponents,
Experience - how many years/fights are behind them, how broad their combat history is,
Hax - how special/unique their abilities are - things you can't "just" brute-force through,
Skills - how wide is their combat toolkit - special attacks, abilities, techniques; Kamehameha, eye lasers, stretching limbs, etc.,
Speed - speed IN COMBAT - reactions and combat movement, not space travel,
Stamina - whether they tire out, whether they need breaks/charge-up windows,
Strength - judged by feats against comparable opponents or scaling,
Style - how consciously built and polished their fighting style is - years of training vs instinct.

Of course, everything will be estimated by my own eye, and these are estimates relative to the opponent, not on some universal scale.

Before we begin, pay attention to the animation in the right panel. It's the metaphor for every hypothetical fight like this. Every fight starts the same way: two characters facing each other. But after that, ALMOST anything can happen. And basically every opinion about a fight between two fictional characters is just personal preference, because until an actual fight happens on the pages of a comic, in a movie, or in a show, everyone will have their own view of who would win.

If you disagree with the stats, the fight scenario, or anything else, you're welcome to discuss it. I always like reading where I made a factual mistake or where my logic completely failed. The comments are yours.

So, first character. In the blue corner, Thragg. In a hypothetical corner, obviously.
I think everyone saw the latest Invincible episode and how hard Thragg dominated every opponent.
And yeah, Thragg is the peak of Viltrumite evolution. Best genes, a specimen selected to become the future Emperor. Trained from birth in every fighting style known to Viltrumites. Thragg hits hard, tears enemies to pieces. And if a fight lasts more than a few seconds, it usually means Thragg is just playing with his food.

And now... advantage.
Thragg's real advantage is his resilience to damage and pain. That's a specific Viltrumite trait - they often keep fighting with their guts hanging out.
I marked it as his edge because, in my opinion, it's the strongest part of Thragg physically. Thragg is fast and strong, sure. But he's more durable than he is fast or strong. That's his strongest card. Paradoxically, because most people would probably say his edge is speed or strength. We'll get to that.

Thragg himself is the opposite of Piccolo in terms of mindset in battle. Thragg usually fights cold, and it's hard to pull him out of that state. Thragg is aware of his advantage over every opponent, and you can feel it in every fight.

--------------

Now the red corner: the red character, rejuvenated Piccolo.

Unlike Thragg, Piccolo doesn't fight in any specific style. Theoretically, Dragon Ball Z: Kakarot says Piccolo fights in "Demon Clan Style," but let's call it no formal style.

Piccolo never had to learn any style, because he always had a gigantic advantage over everyone on Earth. So learning martial arts was unnecessary.

Piccolo's advantage... hard to pick one thing, because there are too many options. Especially compared to someone as one-dimensional as Thragg... But I decided Piccolo's Namekian regeneration should be his edge. If he loses an arm, he can just "spit" a new one out of the stump like nothing happened.

Piccolo is the Demon King... at least in name. Proud, vain, definitely arrogant, but for good reason. Nobody could beat him, except Mafuba, which could seal him, so it's no surprise his ego is high.

--------------

Now let's move to both characters' profiles.

And here you quickly see how completely different they are.

Thragg's profile is very simple. Suspiciously simple for someone this absurdly dangerous.

Thragg has Smart Atoms - the typical Viltrumite adaptive biology that lets them survive insane things: temperature extremes, overload, extreme environments, space flight, that kind of stuff. Plus the standard package: strength, speed, durability, flight.

The problem with Thragg is that he has basically no special tools. Thragg fights exclusively with his own body. No energy techniques, no ranged tricks, nothing that suddenly changes the rules of a fight. Thragg just flies at you, grabs you, hits you, tears you apart, crushes you, done.

And to be clear: in a huge number of matchups, that's absolutely enough. More than enough even - against characters like Invincible or Omni-Man.

That leads directly to Thragg's biggest weakness - and all Viltrumites' weakness: lack of ranged attacks.

And this isn't a funny little weakness like "yeah okay, technically missing something, but who cares." No. It's a real hole in the toolkit. If the opponent can punish from range, control space, and doesn't let himself get dragged into close quarters, Thragg literally has to walk through everything with his face just to start playing his own game.

Sure, in the data you also have things like the Scourge Virus and the Klaxus plant that can kill a Viltrumite. But in practice, for this matchup, those are trivia. What matters more is that Thragg is powerful but narrowly specialized. Almost everything he does, he does brilliantly. He just doesn't do it broadly.

--------------

Now let's look at Piccolo.

Piccolo's profile is the opposite of Thragg's.

Piccolo is also physically very dangerous, but on top of that he has Ki. And in Dragon Ball, Ki isn't just a "hand laser." It's an overall combat multiplier. The higher the power, the higher not only attack strength, but also speed, reactions, and body toughness.

That's important, because many people look at this fight and think: okay, Piccolo has range, Thragg has speed and close combat. To them, the whole fight is "can Thragg close distance?"

In my opinion, that's not it.

Piccolo is dangerous when he's far away and spamming blasts. Piccolo is also dangerous when the fight gets direct. His power scales his whole combat profile.

Then add techniques: Ki blasts, ranged pressure, the ability to hurt the opponent without entering his comfort zone, giant form, stretching limbs. All those weird tools Thragg simply doesn't have and isn't used to.

That doesn't mean Piccolo has no weaknesses.

He does.

First, fighting at full power shortens his life, so in theory he's not a character who can sit at absolute peak forever for free. But just like the Klaxus plant, that's trivia that won't matter here. Losing a month of life, when Demon King Piccolo lives for centuries, is not a combat weakness.

Second, Piccolo is arrogant. And arrogance in fictional fights often means giving initiative away for free. Someone should finish the fight, but instead starts talking, playing with the opponent, or testing how much more he can take. To balance that, Piccolo has strategic sense that Thragg clearly doesn't. Piccolo can return to serious fighting and drop his arrogance when cornered.

Third, Namekian regeneration is not magical immortality. It has limits and slightly weakens the Namekian.

But again, those are more theoretical punishment routes than something Thragg gets handed automatically. To exploit Piccolo's weaknesses, you first have to reach him - and then you still have to hurt him effectively.

Now we get to feats - everyone's favorite part, because this is where descriptions end and concrete evidence begins.

Thragg: a direct bite from Dinosaurus doesn't faze him. The same Dinosaurus that could bite Omnipotus's head off.

Piccolo? Piccolo laughs after taking Goku's Kamehameha.

Back to Thragg: during his fight with Battle Beast, the opponent exposes part of his skull, and Thragg keeps fighting like nothing happened.

Piccolo: Goku himself after the 22nd Budokai says his speed isn't even close to King Piccolo's. Worth noting: in the previous tournament, Krillin, much weaker than Goku, moved so fast he literally vanished from the audience's sight - together with Jackie Chun.

Back to Thragg again: he stops Kregg without much effort when Kregg tries to finish Omni-Man and Invincible.

Piccolo in return creates massive shockwaves with just a hand motion.

And again Thragg: even Conquest - a walking nightmare himself - respects him. Conquest, who conquered countless worlds, fears the Grand Regent.

And again Piccolo: he destroys an entire city with one Ki attack.

The last feat I picked for Thragg was dodging Space Racer's Infinity Ray and catching him. Many people say Thragg's key edge here should be speed, so I had to include it.

Piccolo's final feat: destroying by sight the vessel he was sealed in.

What do all those feats together tell us?
Thragg isn't just "a very strong Viltrumite." He's the Viltrumite who makes everyone else suddenly look less impressive than they seemed.
He doesn't just win. He often wins with the feeling that the opponent was never a truly equal problem.

But things don't get any less absurd on Piccolo's side.
Piccolo's feats say this clearly: this isn't a character who has to touch you to be a lethal threat. He can apply pressure with presence alone, with movement alone, with energy alone. And that's exactly the kind of advantage Thragg hates most, because he can't answer it with his own toolkit.

Now for the second "meat": the win archive, basically each character's CV - who beat whom.

Thragg has Battle Beast on his record - already a cult character hunting an honorable death, similar to Kraven from Spider-Man.

Piccolo's side includes Goku from their first fight. Of course it was a weaker Goku, but Piccolo was also in his old, dying form.

For Thragg we also add Omni-Man and Invincible at once. That's a very nice feat, especially since those two had previously beaten Viltrumite teams.

Piccolo gets seemingly unimpressive Chiaotzu. But paradoxically it's meaningful, because Chiaotzu is living hax. He can read minds, freeze opponents, and even cause brutal constipation - as he proved against Krillin.

Thragg gets Oliver on his CV. Short but informative fight. Oliver basically lost instantly against the Grand Regent.

Piccolo also killed Shenron. Not a strict fight, but destroying the wish-granting Eternal Dragon should be impressive by itself.

Thadeus, another on Thragg's list. Like Oliver, killed with one strike due to Thragg's massive superiority.

Meanwhile Piccolo has a win over Tien. He didn't even need to beat him personally. In battle, King Piccolo spat an egg, from which his child hatched carrying part of Piccolo's power. Tien had no chance against him.

And the final two feats for both characters. I couldn't find any sensible wins at the level of the previous ones, and since I decided every win archive would have 5 entries, I had to find one more each... and I did.

Rognarr for Thragg - a species dangerous to regular Viltrumites - and King Piccolo gets on his CV... the King's Guard, basically an ordinary human.

What do both lists tell us? Both characters almost always fought opponents far below their power level.
Prime Piccolo wasn't just another villain. He was a wall. Something hanging over the entire setting like fate. Exceptional warriors followed for the first 12 Dragon Ball volumes simply did not belong to his league.
Thragg is similar. The story builds power through Omni-Man, then Conquest, and finally shows Thragg's scale: Conquest fears him, and Omni-Man is about as threatening to Thragg as a slice of ham is to my cat.

--------------

Only after going through character profiles, feats and wins, we can start talking numbers. Stats without context are useless.
But if we first see how they fight, what they fight with, what their limits are, and what they've already shown, then the numbers start making sense. At least I think so.

Strength.

Thragg gets 68, Piccolo 84.
And again, I know this can bother some people, because physically Thragg looks like someone who should crush everything barehanded.

But Dragon Ball's scale is simply different. If we accept Ki scaling as it works in that universe, Piccolo's strength doesn't end at flashy explosions. It also translates to pure combat force.

Thragg is insanely strong. But not strong enough to beat Demon King Piccolo in this category. Thragg destroys cities, and Piccolo destroys cities.
But let's look at concrete scaling examples.
Three powerful Viltrumites - Omni-Man, Thadeus, and Invincible - needed Space Racer's help to destroy planet Viltrum through a combined strike on the core. Thragg is stronger than them, true, so MAYBE he could do that alone.

How do we scale Piccolo? Roshi, at around 139 Ki, could destroy the Moon with one Kamehameha. Attack power depends on charge time and probably intent too... one Roshi Kamehameha destroys a mountain, another destroys the Moon. Huge spread.
Piccolo is at least twice as strong as Roshi. That's a massive edge in Dragon Ball.
These are estimates, but to me Piccolo is Dragon Ball's first Planetary+ level character.
True, Piccolo never destroyed a planet, but only because he wanted to rule Earth as Demonic King, not erase it like Beerus or Broly would. Not destroying Earth doesn't mean he couldn't. Since he's far stronger than Roshi, who genuinely feared him, I place him there.

Now speed - probably one of the more controversial categories.
I gave Thragg 64 and Piccolo 78.
And I know some people instinctively want Thragg higher because Viltrumites fly through space, chase ships, react to absurd stuff, etc.

But we need a distinction: travel speed in vacuum is not the same as combat speed, strike speed, or reaction speed.

And here Piccolo is better to me. Not only due to feats, but also Dragon Ball logic where power growth directly boosts whole fight performance: reactions, tempo, timing, entry and exit from exchanges. Thragg is fast, very fast. But not so fast that speed alone strips Piccolo of his biggest edge: range control.
Online you'll often see Thragg listed as FTL+ - faster than light many times over. In travel speed? Probably, because otherwise flying from Viltrum to Earth would likely take thousands of years. Just guessing.
But... if Thragg were that fast, why didn't he catch Omni-Man, Invincible, and Thadeus when they flew to destroy Viltrum? He saw what they were trying to do and flew after them. He was supposedly faster than them and had superior stats across the board.
In both the episode and comic, that trio flies for a while through a tunnel made by Space Racer. A minute? Two? If Thragg is supposedly much faster than light, why didn't he catch them while chasing?
Exactly. Because in Invincible, travel speed most likely works by a Viltrumite accelerating continuously in a straight line until, hypothetically, exceeding light speed.
But accelerating for weeks to reach that speed is not the same as having that speed in a fight where you have half a second to make and finish a move - a dodge or a strike.

Piccolo, meanwhile, has around 260 Ki. Krillin, with around 60 Ki, moved so fast the human eye couldn't track him and he became invisible. No Viltrumite has moved that fast in any fight. Piccolo is much faster than Krillin. Yes, that's scaling-based, but hard to argue Piccolo isn't faster than Krillin.

So the conclusion is: Thragg is faster than Piccolo if they have to fly to Tesco on another planet. But in combat, Piccolo has a gigantic speed advantage.

Next stat: durability.

Very close here: 78 for Thragg, 75 for Piccolo.

This is exactly where the number alone doesn't tell the whole story. Thragg is more durable than Piccolo, and I think that's fair. His body handles brutal damage better, and his pain resistance is monstrous.

But Piccolo isn't far behind, because his durability isn't only raw body toughness - it's also boosted by Ki, and partly by regeneration mitigating damage effects.

Now Battle IQ.

I gave 74 to Thragg and 82 to Piccolo.

And let me be clear: lower Battle IQ for Thragg does not mean Thragg is stupid. Not at all. Thragg is a highly effective fighter, can assess situations coldly, and uses what he has very well. Usually.
But exceptions happen. For example, Thragg fought Battle Beast for many days... Battle Beast, who can't fly. Thragg didn't have to take that fight. He could have thrown him into vacuum and maybe no one would ever find him. Yet he got stuck in an even fight that almost killed him, then needed weeks to recover.
Same with the fight against Invincible on the Sun's surface. Both were burned to the bone and kept fighting... Why did Thragg keep fighting there instead of leaving? Because he was blinded by rage and not thinking rationally. That's low battle IQ.

Piccolo, meanwhile, fought Goku after Kami's training, and the fight was very even. You could even say Piccolo started losing. So he used injured Tien to force Goku into stillness and then broke his limbs from a distance. That's high battle IQ behavior. At that moment Thragg would just hit harder and faster, maybe into his own death. Piccolo, once he understood the result wasn't certain, changed tactics and didn't cling to arrogance.

Now it gets interesting: Hax.

Thragg 22. Piccolo 84.

Nothing to discuss. Thragg is an honest fighter in the most brutal sense possible. He has body, power, speed, durability. That's it. Next to him, Piccolo looks like a toolbox: regeneration, Ki, ranged attacks, weird body properties, giant form, stretching limbs. They may not be strict "hax" in the purest definition, but against someone as straightforward as Thragg? They're practically cosmic abilities beyond Thragg's wildest expectations. That's why the gap is this large.

Okay, stamina.

Here Thragg 88, Piccolo 72.

Thragg's advantage is obvious. Thragg is a stamina monster. He can fight long, hard, and brutally, and it doesn't affect him the way it should affect any living being.

Fighting style.

86 for Thragg and 10 for Piccolo.

Thragg's advantage here is completely natural. He was trained from childhood, and you can feel it. His fighting has structure, discipline, and brutality - but not chaotic brutality unless forced into it. It's learned brutality, tested brutality, and constantly refined brutality.

Compared to that, Piccolo is wild. More intuitive. More based on raw power and unusual tools than on a classically polished martial style.

Experience.

Thragg gets 92, Piccolo 76.

Not much philosophy here. Thragg is older, has lived longer, fought more, and operated in a much harsher, more militarized world. He's a type raised for war - not tournaments, not saving the world, but domination and extermination.

Piccolo obviously has experience too, but next to Thragg it looks modest. Especially if we consider Piccolo spent a significant part of life sealed by Mafuba. And Piccolo often acted through his offspring rather than personally engaging.

Now skills.

Thragg has 58, Piccolo 82.
Again, this follows directly from how these characters are written. Thragg is lethal but his toolkit is narrow: rush in, pressure, tear apart, crush. He does it excellently, but doesn't have many variants.

Piccolo simply has more variants. He has energy techniques capable of city-level destruction without effort.

Let's move to the chart, where it's easier to see who has the edge where.

In average stats, both are ALMOST equal. But that's deceptive, because they sit on two completely different archetype poles.

Thragg wins in durability, stamina, style, and experience. Piccolo wins in strength, speed, battle IQ, hax, and skills. Everything points to Thragg being Piccolo's dream punching bag - one he can beat for a long time and it'll keep coming back for more.

Thragg is the more resilient and more experienced close-combat monster.
Piccolo, meanwhile, has every argument needed to make Thragg's advantages only prolong his suffering.

--------------

So, let's summarize.

In my view, this fight is completely one-sided. It's not a stomp, but only because Piccolo enjoys his victims' suffering. It's not his style to kill a strong opponent immediately. And that's where Thragg is a perfect fit.

Even if Thragg surprises Piccolo, even if you give him first move, Thragg has no option to end this in one strike. Piccolo isn't Thadeus. Piccolo is as durable as he is strong and fast - beyond Thragg's limits.

Piccolo dominates this fight at every range, and in every situation he has both advantage and initiative.

--------------

And now... the most important question: what would this fight look like in practice?

Let's assume any terrain, because location doesn't matter here. But just in case, let's assume they fight on Pine Ave, Little Fishing Lane, Saskaczuan.

In my opinion, the opening is easy to predict.

Thragg moves first.

And it's hard to imagine otherwise. Thragg isn't a character who circles, scouts, checks range, and plays 4-move-ahead chess. Thragg wants his game immediately. He wants to close distance, impose physical pressure, and make the fight dirty, brutal, and direct as fast as possible.

Piccolo has an answer to literally every one of those plans from second one.

If Thragg opens aggressively, Piccolo can just start punishing from range. He doesn't have to go all-in right away. He doesn't have to prove he's stronger right away. It's enough to force Thragg forward under Ki pressure. And that's already unfavorable for a Viltrumite, because every meter Thragg has to cover under fire works in Piccolo's favor.

But let's assume Thragg does what he does best: ignores damage, pushes through, and eventually reaches Piccolo. Still, as both stats and scaling suggest, getting into close range alone changes nothing.
Piccolo is not Omni-Man. Not Invincible. Not someone who automatically loses physically once Thragg gets in.

Piccolo is still a monster in close quarters. Still faster, still stronger, still more combat-flexible, still able to use things Thragg simply doesn't know: stretching limbs, weird attack angles, sudden switches from contact to energy pressure.

In Thragg's best-case scenario, he turns this into a long, attritional brawl.
But even then, his path to victory is invisible.

What happens then? Thragg takes a lot. Piccolo takes hits too, but hits back harder. Thragg wants to stretch the fight because that's natural with his stamina and durability. Piccolo has no major issue with that, because every exchange increasingly reveals the toolkit gap. For most of the fight, Thragg can only keep coming back for more. Piccolo can choose whether to keep it direct or push it back to range and regain full control.

And that's Thragg's biggest tragedy in this matchup.
He has no Plan B.

If the first wave of brutal pressure doesn't finish it, his options don't really expand later. He keeps doing the same thing, only more desperately. Piccolo is the opposite. The longer it goes, the more hooks he gets. The better he reads rhythm. The easier he can choose how to run the fight.

Can Thragg look dangerous in this fight?
Yes. Very dangerous, even.

He can enter hard. He can land shots. He can surprise Piccolo with aggression. He can make viewers briefly feel this might be closer than expected.

But to me, that's only Act One, not a result reversal.
Because very quickly, every road leads to the same place.
If Thragg pressures, Piccolo punishes with Ki.
If Thragg reaches close range, Piccolo absorbs contact without losing control and returns heavier shots.
If Thragg tries to reset and breathe, he gives up all space to an opponent who feels even better at range.

And that's how we reach the endgame.
The endgame, in my view, doesn't depend on one specific finish.
And that's important: when one character has one meaningful win condition and the other has several, the matchup is simply hostile to the first one.

Let's look at the fight simulation itself.
In those wonderful animations that took me 80 hours to prepare, we see - in simplified form - how the hypothetical scenario could play out.

Piccolo can close the fight with a Ki barrage.
He can close it by breaking Thragg in direct exchanges.

That's exactly why I'd call it one-sided, but not a "stomp" in the simplest sense.
A stomp implies immediate execution, and I don't see that here. Not because Thragg is that close to Piccolo's level, but because psychologically Piccolo isn't the type who instantly deletes every opponent by the fastest available method. If he has someone exceptionally tough in front of him, it's very likely he'll want to enjoy that advantage for a while.

And that's exactly what saves Thragg from total humiliation in the first two seconds.

Not stats.
Not style.
Not experience.
Only Piccolo's personality.

--------------

And finally, La Grande Finale. Final verdict.

My verdict is simple.
Piccolo 10 out of 10.

You can give Thragg an element of surprise, even a few early clean shots. The guy has absurd durability and stamina anyone could envy.
But he has no real win condition. No tools to take permanent control of the fight's flow. No answer to Ki pressure. No meaningful ranged argument. And his offensive ceiling is lower than Piccolo's.

Piccolo, meanwhile, has everything needed to run this fight his way from start to finish.

He has greater strength.
He has greater combat speed.
He has a larger arsenal.
He has greater hax.
He has better Battle IQ.
And most importantly, he has more than one way to close the fight.

That's exactly why Piccolo is the absolute favorite here.
Thragg can make this fight spectacular.
But I see no convincing reason why he should win it.
